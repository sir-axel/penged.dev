House rules

01

Every player signs off

A win only counts once each participant accepts the submitted result. Doubles needs all four. Declined or disputed matches never touch your rating.

02

Rating is not level

Rating measures who you beat and moves only on verified results. Level is earned by showing up and playing — it never buys you rank.

03

Challenges, not shortcuts

You climb by beating the player above you. A challenge has a 72-hour window, and silence never hands anyone a rank automatically.

Ratings

How ratings work

Elo, like chess ratings. Your rating moves only once everyone who played has confirmed the result, so nobody can award themselves a win.

The short version

  • Everyone starts at 1400.
  • Beating someone above you gains more than beating someone below you.
  • Losing to a stronger opponent costs fewer points.
  • K-factor: 48 for your first 10 matches, 32 until 30, then 24 for good.
  • One result moves you at most 45 points, and no rating ever drops below 700.
  • Rematches are worth less — every earlier meeting with the same opponent halves the gain, and after six in six months a win pays nothing.
  • Margins don't matter: 11-0 and 11-9 count exactly the same.

The maths

expected = 1 / (1 + 10 ^ ((opponentRating - yourRating) / 400))
newRating = yourRating + K * (result - expected) * repeatFactor

result       = 1 for a win, 0 for a loss
K            = 48 (first 10) / 32 (next 20) / 24 (after)
repeatFactor = 1 / (1 + 0.75 * earlier meetings)

Level is not rating

Level counts the work you put in. Rating counts who you beat — so grinding easy games never buys rank.

  • +25 xp for turning up
  • +25 xp for winning
  • +5 xp per game you take (five games max)

level = floor(sqrt(xp / 40)) + 1 · level 5 opens at 640 xp

Worked example

In your first match, a 1400-rated player beats a 1600-rated one. Expected score is 0.24, so the win is worth 48 × (1 − 0.24) = 36 points: the winner moves to 1436 and, because the win came against a higher rank, also takes that spot on the ladder.
